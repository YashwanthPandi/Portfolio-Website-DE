Welcome to Day 1 of your journey.

If you are here, you’re likely excited to start "spinning up resources" in the Cloud or writing Python scripts. But here is the truth: **The most expensive mistake a Data Engineer can make is building the wrong thing efficiently.**

Before we touch the keyboard, we need to master the art of translation.

## The Requirements Hierarchy

In data engineering, "requirements" isn't a single item—it’s a hierarchy. To deliver value, you must understand all three levels:

| Level | Goal | Responsibility | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Business** | High-level strategy | Executives | "We need to grow revenue by 10% this year." |
| **Stakeholder** | Individual needs | End Users | "I need a dashboard to see which products are failing." |
| **System** | Technical specs | **You (The Engineer)** | "The system must update the SQL database every 4 hours." |



---

## The "What" vs. The "How"

Once you understand the business goal, you must define the **System Requirements**. We split these into two buckets: **Functional** and **Non-Functional**.

### 1. Functional Requirements (The "What")
These are the specific features the system must have. If the system doesn't do these, it is "broken."
* **Alerting:** Notify users when there is an anomaly in the data.
* **Updates:** Provide regular updates to an analytics database.
* **Ingestion:** Pull data from the e-commerce API every hour.

### 2. Non-Functional Requirements (The "How")
These are the constraints. They define how the system performs, its security, and its cost.
* **Scalability:** Must handle 10x traffic during Black Friday.
* **Security:** Data must be encrypted and follow regulatory requirements (GDPR).
* **Budget:** Monthly cloud costs must stay under $500.

### Your First Meeting: 10 Discovery Questions
When you meet your stakeholders (like the Data Science team), they won't speak in SQL or Python. They speak in "pains." Use these questions to find the real requirements:

* **Objective:** What business decision will this data help you make?
* **Source:** Where does the raw data live currently (API, SQL, S3)?
* **Freshness:** How often does the data need to be updated (Daily, Hourly, Real-time)?
* **Volume:** How much data are we moving (Gigabytes or Terabytes)?
* **Quality:** What does "clean" data look like to you?
* **Audience:** Who else is going to look at this output?
* **Format:** Do you need this in a SQL table, a flat file, or a BI tool?
* **Retention:** Do we need to keep historical snapshots or just the current state?
* **Reliability:** What happens to the business if this data is 2 hours late?
* **Security:** Is there sensitive PII (Personally Identifiable Information) in here?

### The Engineering Value Formula

We can mathematically represent the value we deliver to a company. As an engineer, your goal is to maximize the numerator while keeping the denominator under control.

$$ \text{Value} = \frac{\text{Reliability} \times (\text{Functional Needs met})}{\text{Cloud Cost} + \text{Technical Debt}} $$

If your system is functional but costs too much to run, or if it's cheap but unreliable, the Value drops to zero.
